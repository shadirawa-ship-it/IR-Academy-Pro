⚡ Catheterization Lab Safety

Comprehensive safety protocols and guidelines for interventional radiology procedures in the catheterization laboratory.

☢️ Radiation Safety

  • Time: Minimize fluoroscopy time; use pulsed fluoroscopy (low pulse rate) instead of continuous imaging. Track cumulative dose per procedure.
  • Distance: Maintain maximum distance from radiation source. Use C-arm positioning to maximize distance while maintaining image quality.
  • Shielding: Use lead aprons (0.5-1 mm Pb equivalent), thyroid shields, and pelvic shields for operators. Position mobile shielding barriers between operator and patient.
  • Dose Tracking: Monitor patient Dose Area Product (DAP) and cumulative effective dose (mGy). Alert patients/physicians of high-dose procedures.
  • Documentation: Record fluoroscopy time and DAP on all procedures. Track annual staff exposure with dosimetry badges.
  • Pregnant Operators: Pregnant staff must declare pregnancy and reduce exposure; use fetal dosimetry monitoring (acceptable limit: 0.5 mSv/month).
  • ALARA Principle: As Low As Reasonably Achievable - always optimize dose reduction without compromising diagnostic quality.

🛡️ Patient Safety

  • Informed Consent: Discuss procedure risks, benefits, alternatives. Document consent process and patient understanding.
  • Allergy Screening: Identify contrast allergy, iodine allergy, shellfish allergy, medication allergies (ACE inhibitors, metformin). Have premedication protocol ready.
  • Renal Function: Check baseline creatinine/eGFR before contrast administration. Consider nephroprotective measures (hydration, N-acetylcysteine) in at-risk patients.
  • Anticoagulation Management: Verify coagulation status; manage antiplatelet/anticoagulant therapy per institutional protocol (aspirin, clopidogrel, warfarin, DOAC).
  • Positioning Safety: Ensure proper patient positioning; pad pressure points to prevent pressure injuries. Maintain airway access and patient monitoring throughout.
  • Contrast Overload: Limit contrast volume (typically ≤3 mL/kg; reduce in renal impairment). Use iso-osmolar or low-osmolar contrast agents.
  • Oxygen Saturation: Maintain SpO2 >94% throughout procedure. Use supplemental oxygen during sedation; monitor capnography if available.
  • Post-Procedure Monitoring: Monitor vitals, bleeding, compartment syndrome (limb procedures), contrast extravasation, allergic reactions.

🔧 Equipment Safety

  • Equipment Maintenance: Perform daily safety checks on fluoroscopy unit, injector, monitors, anesthesia machine. Document all maintenance and repairs.
  • Emergency Equipment: Verify crash cart, defibrillator, intubation kit, suction are present and functional before each case.
  • Device Integrity: Inspect all catheters, guidewires, sheaths for damage, kinking, or contamination before use. Check expiration dates on all devices.
  • Sterilization: Verify proper sterilization of reusable instruments; use only sterile devices for procedures. Maintain sterile field throughout case.
  • Line Management: Secure all catheters/sheaths; prevent entanglement and accidental dislodgement. Use appropriate flush solutions (heparinized saline).
  • Infection Prevention: Use aseptic technique at all access sites. Change contaminated drapes or fields immediately.
  • Fire Safety: Know location of fire extinguishers. Be aware of fire risk with oxygen and electrosurgical devices. Have fire response plan.
  • Mechanical Complications: Monitor for guidewire breakage, catheter perforation, contrast extravasation. Have immediate intervention plan for complications.

🧤 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

  • Lead Apron: Wear 0.5-1 mm Pb equivalent lead apron during fluoroscopy. Check for cracks/holes regularly (replace if damaged). Proper storage prevents deterioration.
  • Thyroid Shield: Use thyroid collar (0.25 mm Pb) to protect thyroid from scatter radiation. Especially critical for operators facing patient.
  • Gloves: Sterile surgical gloves for all sterile procedures. Double glove if high perforation risk. Latex-free available for allergic staff/patients.
  • Gowns & Aprons: Waterproof gown or apron to prevent blood/fluid contamination. Fluid-resistant material required for procedures with anticipated spillage.
  • Head/Face Protection: Surgical cap to prevent hair/scalp contamination. Face shield or protective glasses for high-splash risk procedures.
  • Masks: N95 or surgical mask depending on procedure type and institution protocol. FFP2 (equivalent to N95) for aerosol-generating procedures.
  • Foot Protection: Closed-toe shoes with fluid-resistant covering. Shoe covers if high contamination risk.
  • Dosimetry Badge: All fluoroscopy staff must wear whole-body dosimetry badge (worn outside lead apron) and optional extremity badge on dominant hand.

💊 Medication Safety

  • Verification: Double-check all medications before administration (right drug, right dose, right route, right patient, right time). Verify with independent second person.
  • Contrast Preparation: Verify contrast type (ionic vs non-ionic), concentration, osmolality. Use appropriate volume based on patient weight/renal function.
  • Sedatives: Have reversal agents available (flumazenil for benzodiazepines, naloxone for opioids). Titrate to effect; monitor continuously during administration.
  • Anticoagulation: Heparin dosing based on weight/ACT (activated clotting time). Protamine available for reversal. Monitor ACT every 30-60 minutes during case.
  • Vasodilators: Nitroglycerin, verapamil for vasospasm; have vasopressor (phenylephrine, dopamine) for hypotension. Know appropriate doses and contraindications.
  • Emergency Medications: Epinephrine (0.1-0.5 mg IV/IO), atropine (0.5-1 mg), amiodarone (300 mg IV), lidocaine (100 mg) for ACLS protocols immediately accessible.
  • Adverse Reactions: Have protocol for contrast reaction (mild/moderate/severe). Steroids, antihistamines, epinephrine ready. Observe all patients post-procedure.
  • Storage & Expiration: Store medications at correct temperature. Check expiration dates; discard expired medications immediately. Organize crash cart medications clearly.
  • Documentation: Record all medications administered, times, doses, patient response. Include any adverse reactions or complications.

🔄 Interventional Radiology Department Workflow

Standard workflow and process map for IR department operations, from patient referral through post-procedure follow-up.

📥 Pre-Procedure Phase (Referral & Scheduling)

  1. Referral Receipt: Receive procedure request from referring physician (electronically, phone, or paper). Verify patient identification and insurance.
  2. Chart Review: Radiologist reviews imaging, labs, clinical indication. Determine if imaging/workup needed before procedure.
  3. Insurance Verification: Verify insurance coverage for proposed procedure. Obtain prior authorization if required.
  4. Patient Communication: Schedule appointment; provide pre-procedure instructions (NPO status, medication adjustments, arrival time). Obtain contact number.
  5. Clinic Visit (if indicated): Nurse/coordinator interviews patient. Assess risk factors, allergies, medications, renal function. Obtain informed consent discussion form.
  6. Prep Orders: Enter lab orders (CBC, PT/INR, creatinine if not recent). Request imaging review (CT/MRI/ultrasound as needed for case planning).
  7. Case Planning Conference: Attending/fellow reviews case; discusses approach, technique, equipment, staffing needs with team.

🏥 Day-of-Procedure (Arrival & Preparation)

  1. Patient Check-In: Verify insurance, collect co-pays, confirm NPO status and medication compliance. Issue hospital wristband with two identifiers.
  2. Pre-Procedure Assessment: Nursing staff obtains vital signs, weight (for dosing), IV access. Review medications (hold metformin if contrast planned; resume 48h post-procedure).
  3. Informed Consent: Interventional radiologist discusses procedure, risks, benefits, alternatives with patient. Obtain signed consent. Answer all patient questions.
  4. Medication Administration: Give pre-medications (antibiotics if needed, premedication for contrast allergy if applicable). Document administration and patient response.
  5. Imaging Review: Technologist/radiologist reviews recent imaging to confirm diagnosis and plan procedure approach.
  6. Transport to Procedure Room: Nursing escorts patient to IR suite. Verify patient identity at each transition point.
  7. Positioning & Monitoring: Position patient per procedure needs. Establish monitoring (ECG, pulse ox, BP cuff, capnography if sedation). Obtain baseline vitals.

🔧 Intra-Procedure Phase (Case Execution)

  1. Time-Out: Entire team (IR physician, fellow, nurse, tech) confirms patient identity, procedure, site (if applicable). Discuss case plan and anticipated issues.
  2. Sterile Field Setup: Technologist and nurse prepare sterile field; open devices and equipment per procedure needs.
  3. Access: IR physician obtains vascular or percutaneous access per technique (Seldinger, ultrasound-guided, fluoroscopy-guided).
  4. Imaging & Navigation: Real-time imaging (fluoroscopy, ultrasound, CT) guides catheter/device placement. Technologist manages image acquisition and recording.
  5. Device Deployment: Place intervention device (stent, coil, drain, catheter) per manufacturer IFU and institutional protocol.
  6. Endpoint Assessment: Perform angiography or final imaging to confirm successful intervention. Look for complications (perforation, dissection, extravasation).
  7. Closure: Remove catheters/sheaths. Achieve hemostasis (manual compression, closure device, sutures). Apply sterile dressing.
  8. Documentation: Complete procedure note (approach, devices used, findings, any complications, fluid/medication administration).

🚑 Post-Procedure Phase (Recovery & Discharge)

  1. Recovery Room Transition: Transport patient to recovery/PACU. Hand-off report includes procedure details, complications, anesthesia drugs used, bleeding status.
  2. Vital Signs Monitoring: Nursing monitors BP, heart rate, SpO2, mental status, pain level every 15 minutes. Call physician if abnormal values.
  3. Access Site Assessment: Frequent checks for bleeding, hematoma, compartment syndrome, nerve injury. Document distal pulses and sensation.
  4. Medication Management: Administer post-op medications (antibiotics, antiemetics, analgesics). Resume home medications per protocol.
  5. Fluid/Hydration: IV fluids if NPO; encourage oral intake as tolerated. Monitor urine output (especially with contrast administration).
  6. Imaging Review: Radiologist reviews procedure images; prepares final report. Notifies clinical team of critical findings.
  7. Discharge Criteria: Stable vitals, alert and oriented (if sedated), no active bleeding, pain controlled, able to void (if urinary catheter not placed). Provide discharge instructions.
  8. Patient Discharge: Provide written discharge summary, medications, activity restrictions, follow-up imaging orders, contact phone number for questions.

📞 Post-Procedure Follow-Up (24h-12 weeks)

  1. 24-Hour Check-In: Nurse calls patient to assess for complications (fever, bleeding, swelling, pain, numbness). Reinforce activity restrictions and medication compliance.
  2. Clinic Visit (if indicated): Follow-up appointment per procedure type (1-2 weeks typical). Assess access site, distal perfusion, wound healing. Address patient concerns.
  3. Imaging Follow-Up: Schedule follow-up imaging (ultrasound, CT, angiography) per protocol. For example: duplex ultrasound at 1-6 months for vascular interventions.
  4. Outcomes Tracking: Document patency, clinical success, long-term complications. Feed data back to IR quality committee.
  5. Referrer Communication: Send final report and follow-up plan to referring physician. Discuss results and next steps (medical management, additional intervention, repeat procedure).
  6. Complication Management: If complications develop (thrombosis, restenosis, infection), escalate to IR physician and clinical team for management plan.
  7. Patient Education: Provide resources on condition management, when to seek emergency care, lifestyle modifications as indicated.

🏢 Department Operations & Quality

  • Staffing: Adequate physician (attending/fellows), nursing, technologist, and anesthesia coverage throughout operating hours.
  • Equipment Maintenance: Regular service contracts; preventive maintenance logs; emergency backup equipment available.
  • Quality Metrics: Track procedure volume, complication rates, patient satisfaction, turnaround times, imaging quality. Monthly quality meetings to review data.
  • Case Reviews: Morbidity & mortality (M&M) conference discusses unexpected outcomes or complications. Identify process improvements.
  • Continuing Education: Regular departmental conferences; journal clubs; vendor in-services on new devices. Document CME attendance.
  • Credentialing: Maintain practitioner credentials; annual privileging verification; documentation of competency (case logs, board certification).
  • Safety Culture: Encourage error reporting via anonymous reporting system. Create action items for system improvements; never punitive response to good-faith errors.
  • Regulatory Compliance: Maintain HIPAA compliance, incident reporting, Joint Commission accreditation standards. Regular compliance audits.
